How it runs

1234567891011121314 1 2 3 4

  1. Day 1 · The briefing

    Bring everything: logs, tickets, the chat thread from two years ago and the colleague who says it works on their machine. Especially them.

  2. Days 2–8 · The legwork

    We reproduce the problem before we touch it. Traces, timelines, a few uncomfortable questions for the codebase. Half the job, and it shows on the calendar.

  3. Days 9–12 · The fix

    One change at a time, each with a test, so everyone knows which one mattered. No heroic rewrites.

  4. Days 13–14 · The handover

    A short report, a walkthrough call and the next three suspects, in case you want to keep going.

Shaded days are weekends. The detective rests; cold cases, famously, don't.

What you get

A case file, not a slide deck. It says what happened, why it kept happening and what stops it from coming back, in plain language and in that order.

Questions

What if you don't find it?

You don't pay the second half, and you get everything we ruled out, which is often half the answer. The detective also takes it personally, for free.

Can you fix it in a week?

We can usually find it in a week. Fixing it properly depends on how much has been built on top of it since.

Will the report blame someone?

It names causes, not people. Bugs this old are rarely one person's fault; they're everyone's habit.

Do you need access to production?

Read-only, and only to what you choose. Most cold cases crack in a copy of production anyway.
